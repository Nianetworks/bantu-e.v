An income of your own means the freedom to decide. With this initiative we strengthen the economic independence of groups that are particularly often excluded: **women’s groups and cooperatives** and **organisations of persons with disabilities**.

## How it works

1. The groups receive **goats and other livestock**.
2. They keep and **breed the animals**: offspring can be sold or passed on to other members.
3. Sales and offspring create an **income of their own**, managed by the groups themselves.

In this way one-off support grows into a lasting, self-managed livelihood.

## Uganda livelihoods programme launched in 2017

Working through women’s groups and our Ugandan partner Anchored Bridges Initiatives (ABI), formerly Ability Beyond the Iris, the initiative supported goat rearing, pig rearing and beekeeping to strengthen family incomes. The programme directly supported 50 families. The project team reports that more than 26 of those families moved out of poverty. The work also aimed to ease financial pressures within households and contribute to the prevention of domestic violence.

### Income generation through women’s groups

ABI worked with supporters to provide livelihood opportunities that families could develop within their communities. Support focused on productive assets and small enterprises that could be managed locally. Women’s groups received support for goat rearing, pig rearing and beekeeping; the women’s role as producers and organisers was central to the programme.

### Goats and pigs as household assets

The photographic archive shows women with goats, livestock shelters and pig-rearing activities. Together, these images document the practical setting in which households cared for their animals and developed income-generating activities. The programme’s livelihood objective was to help families build a more reliable economic base; its wider social aim was to reduce financial strain and support safer family relationships.

### Beekeeping and continued family support

Beekeeping complemented the livestock activities. The archive includes traditional hives among trees and a project visit to a beekeeping site. The reported progress of more than 26 families provides a reason to continue supporting women’s economic opportunities while following how income and family wellbeing develop over time.

We thank the women’s groups, participating families, ABI and the supporters who made these livelihood opportunities possible. The figures describe the historical Uganda initiative and are reported by the project team.
