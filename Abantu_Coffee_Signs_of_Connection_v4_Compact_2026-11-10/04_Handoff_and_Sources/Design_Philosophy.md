# Signs of Connection

Identity is carried by scale, rhythm and meaning. A single strong graphic leads each composition; small supporting marks create a second reading rather than a decorative frame. The whole surface is considered, including folds and side panels, so the artwork belongs to the object.

Colour distinguishes personalities through proportion as much as hue. Cream and cobalt can be spacious and directional; green and ivory can be dense and rooted; clay, plum, mustard and cyan carry other rhythms. Meticulous restraint keeps each palette coherent and prevents expressive marks from becoming visual noise.

Typography supplies a stable voice across different compositions. A firm geometric wordmark, an open product name and a quieter factual line form a carefully calibrated hierarchy. Original owner and partner marks are retained with precise proportions and generous clear space, at an endorsement scale.

Some visual ideas are grounded in named Adinkra forms; others are new graphic compositions. A Kwanzaa-inspired design draws on unity and communal foundation. Their sources and meanings remain explicit, with no suggestion that every reference shares one geography or tradition. The product origin and the artistic reference are different pieces of information.

The craft lies in the relationship between boldness and silence: well-placed counters, exact repetition, deliberate cropping and an unforced balance of shape and type. Painstaking spacing and masterful editing should make each edition unmistakable, while the family remains recognisable.
