The story of Roots & Routes

Sankofa is an image from the Akan culture of West Africa: a bird turning its head to look back. It carries the idea that learning from the past guides us forward. Roots & Routes links this idea with the people who grow coffee and with the diaspora who drink it.

The coffee comes from the slopes of Mount Elgon, from 1,800 to 2,300 metres above sea level and from volcanic soils. Our main partner Balambuli Kwigate Coffee (BKC) works there with smallholder farmers. A fermentation of 16 to 40 hours is planned.

Later, video stories from the farmers and news from the Community Resource Centre are meant to be found on this page as well.

The label

On the label the Sankofa bird looks back: an Adinkra sign from the Akan culture of Ghana, in a contemporary interpretation. It stands for learning from the past. The sign is not a Ugandan symbol; it stands for the idea of the edition, not for the origin of the coffee.

The back is printed in German. It gives the coffee details, tells the story in brief and names the aims of the edition: fairer incomes for coffee-farming families, training and better educational opportunities. It also carries the storage advice (keep cool, dry and away from light; close well after opening) and a QR code that leads to this page. The fields for best-before date, batch and roaster are still empty; they will only be filled in for the actual production run.

What this edition aims to support

  • Fair prices for coffee cherries and seasonal bonuses for farmers
  • Training and equipment for farming
  • Digital literacy at the Community Resource Centre
  • Access to healthcare and scholarships for children from farming families

These are aims, not results already achieved. No fixed share per pack has been set.
