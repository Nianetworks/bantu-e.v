Anchored Bridges Initiatives Uganda

Six reports from the ABI project archive

Livelihoods, inclusive education, mobility support and women’s craft enterprise. Anchored Bridges Initiatives was formerly known as Ability Beyond the Iris.

Livestock and agriculture for family livelihoods

The livelihoods programme worked through women’s groups to strengthen household income through goats, pigs and beekeeping. It directly supported 50 families. The project team reports that more than 26 of those families moved out of poverty. The work also aimed to ease financial pressures within households and contribute to the prevention of domestic violence.

School solidarity between Bendorf and Uganda

Pupils at Wilhelm-Remy-Gymnasium Bendorf raised approximately €7,000 to support fellow learners in Uganda through Anchored Bridges Initiatives (ABI), formerly Ability Beyond the Iris. Their support helped provide school materials and Christmas gift boxes and finance two access ramps at Royal College Namugongo. The partnership also brought learning about Uganda into English classes in Bendorf.

Mobility aids for participation in Uganda

In 2018, the initiative mobilised two 40-foot containers of mobility equipment and distributed aids to more than 600 beneficiaries in Ntungamo, Jinja and Kasubi. Working with ABI, Crutches for Africa and individual supporters in Germany, the project connected donated equipment with people who needed it for everyday movement, education and work.

Craft enterprise for women with disabilities

Since 2018, the organisation has supported women living with different disabilities to develop income and employment through craft production. Working with ABI, the initiative has supported a small number of producers, purchased their products and helped find buyers in Europe. Its focus is on recognising women as skilled producers and strengthening opportunities for paid work.

Supporting school meals at Rwengoma Primary

Through ABI, the initiative contributed UGX 1,000,000 to Rwengoma Primary School to support its lower primary lunch programme and its plan to establish a school vegetable garden. The contribution came from the approximately €7,000 raised by pupils at Wilhelm-Remy-Gymnasium Bendorf. It responded to concerns that hunger was among the reasons pupils were leaving school.

School fee support for six Royal Junior pupils

Six pupils at Royal Junior Namugongo received school-fee support when their families could not fully finance their education. Support was delivered through ABI using funds raised by pupils at Wilhelm-Remy-Gymnasium Bendorf. The project account reports that all six pupils completed primary education and progressed to secondary school.
